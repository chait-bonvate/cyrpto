THE BASICS

What is Cryptography?

Cryptography is the science and practice of protecting information by transforming it into a form that unauthorized people cannot understand.

The word comes from the Greek words kryptos, meaning hidden, and graphein, meaning writing.

Modern cryptography uses mathematics, algorithms and computational techniques to protect digital information during storage and communication.

01

Confidentiality

Ensures that only authorized people can access information.

02

Integrity

Helps detect whether information has been modified.

03

Authentication

Verifies the identity of users and systems.

04

Non-Repudiation

Provides evidence that a particular action or message originated from a specific party.

THE EVOLUTION

A Journey Through Time

~100 BCE

Caesar Cipher

Julius Caesar reportedly used a substitution technique that shifted letters by a fixed number of positions.

1500s

Vigenère Cipher

A polyalphabetic substitution cipher that used multiple Caesar-like shifts controlled by a key.

1940s

Enigma

Electromechanical rotor machines were used to encrypt military communications during World War II.

1970s

Modern Cryptography

Public-key cryptography and standards such as DES transformed digital security.

Today

Digital Security

Algorithms such as AES, RSA, ECC and SHA are used across modern computing and communications.

CORE CONCEPTS

Methods of Cryptography

🔐
01

Symmetric Encryption

The same secret key is used to encrypt and decrypt information.

AES · ChaCha20
🔑
02

Asymmetric Encryption

Uses a pair of mathematically related keys: a public key and a private key.

RSA · ECC
#
03

Hashing

Converts data into a fixed-length digest designed to make reversing the original input impractical.

SHA-256 · SHA-3
✍
04

Digital Signatures

Uses cryptographic techniques to verify the origin and integrity of digital messages or documents.

RSA-PSS · ECDSA

LOOKING AHEAD

The Future of Cryptography

Preparing for a Post-Quantum World

Cryptography continues to evolve as computing technology changes. Large-scale quantum computers could threaten some widely used public-key algorithms.

Researchers are therefore developing post-quantum cryptography algorithms designed to resist attacks from both classical and quantum computers.

01

Quantum Resistance

New algorithms are being developed to withstand potential quantum attacks.

02

AI & Security

Artificial intelligence may influence both cryptanalysis and security engineering.
